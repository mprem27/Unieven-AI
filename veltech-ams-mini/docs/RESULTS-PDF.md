# Download a semester statement

1. Sign in as a student and open **Academics → Results & grades**.
2. Choose the semester. Optionally choose its source report, examination session or subject filter.
3. Choose **Semester PDF**. Your browser downloads the PDF and records a device-local copy under **Settings → Downloads**, subject to available device storage. Choose **Export Excel** for an ordinary workbook with separate source-report sheets.

The university heading uses two balanced centred lines. Student labels share the table margin; name/roll fields have consistent baselines. Headers and data cells are vertically centred, with wrapped long course names and proportional summary columns. Both tables use the same page edges.

The PDF follows the supplied reference: A4 university heading/emblem, programme, Statement of Marks/Grade, semester/session, official registration number, name and roll number, college/department, subjects with grade/points/credits, and the semester summary. Source attempts and examinations are separate groups; long statements continue onto another page. Filters appear in the statement when it includes only part of a report.

Only the five required official identity fields are used: name, registration number, roll number, degree and department. Editable social details, email, parents, portrait and private account metadata are not exported. The marksheet reference's student's identity and marks are not bundled in this project. Excel exports contain source rows and omit the student's profile.

Listed source credits and credit points are totalled only when complete and unambiguous. GPA/CGPA and other summary cells use the corresponding published semester values; missing or conflicting values stay blank. A global CGPA is not assigned to a semester whose CGPA was not published. The footer identifies the document as a student-generated copy, without an official signature, seal of attestation or claim of university issuance.

The PDF's built-in Times fonts support Latin text. Excel preserves Unicode names/course text. Downloads are private files on the student's device; the student controls further sharing.
